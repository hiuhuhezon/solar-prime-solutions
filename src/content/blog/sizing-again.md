## What's available in Kenya?

Walk into any solar shop in Nairobi, and you'll see at least five different battery types. Here's what they actually mean for your home.

### Lithium-Ion (LiFePO4) — The Best Choice

Lithium batteries are the gold standard. They last 10–15 years, you can use up to 80% of their capacity, and they need zero maintenance. They're expensive upfront but cheaper over time because you won't replace them every few years.

**Pros:** Long lifespan, deep discharge, lightweight, efficient, no maintenance.  
**Cons:** Higher upfront cost.  
**Price:** KSh 180,000 – 400,000 for 5kWh

### Lead-Acid — The Budget Option

Lead-acid batteries are the cheapest to buy, but they don't last. You can only use 50% of their capacity, and they need regular maintenance. In Kenya's heat, they typically die after 3–5 years. Over 15 years, you'll buy them three or four times.

**Pros:** Cheap upfront, widely available.  
**Cons:** Short life, heavy, needs maintenance, only 50% usable.  
**Price:** KSh 50,000 – 120,000 for 5kWh

### Gel Batteries — The Middle Ground

Gel batteries are maintenance-free and last longer than standard lead-acid. They're a good compromise if you can't afford lithium yet. But they still only give you 50% usable capacity and need to be replaced every 5–7 years.

**Pros:** Maintenance-free, spill-proof.  
**Cons:** Still limited to 50% discharge, slower charging.  
**Price:** KSh 80,000 – 180,000 for 5kWh

## How much battery do you need?

Here's a simple formula:

**Battery size = Daily consumption × Days of backup ÷ Depth of Discharge**

If you use 8 kWh per day and want one day of backup with lithium (80% DoD):

**8 × 1 ÷ 0.8 = 10 kWh**

With lead-acid (50% DoD), you'd need twice as much:

**8 × 1 ÷ 0.5 = 16 kWh**

That's why lithium is more cost-effective — you need less battery to do the same job.

## What does it cost over time?

| Battery type | Upfront cost | Lifespan | Cost over 15 years |
|-------------|-------------|----------|-------------------|
| Lithium | KSh 350,000 | 15 years | KSh 350,000 |
| Lead-Acid | KSh 120,000 | 4 years | KSh 450,000 |
| Gel | KSh 180,000 | 6 years | KSh 450,000 |

Lithium is more expensive upfront but cheaper in the long run. This is the math most installers don't show you.

## Which one should you buy?

**If you have the budget:** Go lithium (LiFePO4). Brands like Pylontech, Dyness, and Huawei are reliable and widely available. It's the best investment you'll make.

**If you're on a tight budget:** Buy gel batteries. They're better than flooded lead-acid and you can upgrade to lithium later.

**If you're off-grid:** Lithium is almost mandatory. You need the deep discharge capability and reliability.

**If you're renting:** Consider a small lithium battery or skip storage entirely and go grid-tied.

  <strong>Important:</strong> Never mix battery types or brands. Always buy matched batteries from the same manufacturer and replace them all at once.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Buying on price alone.** The cheapest battery is rarely the best value. Look at total cost of ownership — lifespan, maintenance, efficiency — not just the sticker price.

**Undersizing your battery.** If you can't afford enough capacity, buy fewer lithium batteries rather than cheap lead-acid. You can always add more later.

**Over-discharging lead-acid.** Draining them below 50% regularly will kill them in a year. It's a waste of money.

**Ignoring installation.** Keep batteries cool and ventilated. Heat is their biggest enemy in Kenya.

## The bottom line

Lithium (LiFePO4) is the clear winner for most homes. It costs more upfront but saves you money over time. It's reliable, maintenance-free, and lasts longer than any other option.

If you can't afford lithium, buy gel batteries. They're the best of the lead-acid options and you can upgrade later.

Whatever you choose, buy from a reputable installer. A good installation is worth paying for.
